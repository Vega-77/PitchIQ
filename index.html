How a match works

STEP 01

Tag from the touchline

One tap per event. The app asks who a corner was awarded to, which card was shown and who assisted — so the record still makes sense a month later. Works with no signal; taps sync when it returns.

STEP 02

Check the numbers

Team totals, a full timeline, and minutes played per player — correct across substitutions and re-entry, which is where hand-kept stats usually go wrong.

STEP 03

Publish reports

Each player gets their own private page. They see their goals, assists, cards and minutes — and never a teammate's.

What's working today

A row is a claim. Open one and it shows the measurement it rests on.

Live tagging, stats and player reports Ready

The half of this that needs no camera at all. One person taps what happens, on a phone or a tablet at the side of the pitch, and the team stats and every player’s own page come out of that log — footage or no footage.

Nine types, and each one states what picking a team asserts — awarded to, committed by, shown to — so a tag still means one thing a month later.

Player detection from match footage Found in 99% of frames on real video Ready

What decides this is where the camera stands, not which model runs. The same detector, with no tuning, was pointed at two real recordings of the same team:

Tight, pitch-filling framing 99%
Wide stadium panorama 0%

300 sampled frames from each clip. At the wide framing a player is four to eight pixels across and not one on the field was found — cropping and upscaling does not bring them back, because the pixels were never recorded.

Ball tracking 83% of frames after dedicated tracking Ready

The ball is the hardest object on the pitch: small, fast, and often half a player. Feeding its detections through the same tracker the players use threw nearly all of them away.

Raw detections 60%
Through the players’ tracker 1.6%
One path through the whole clip 83%

Measured on 30 seconds of real footage. That tracker only confirms a track when detections line up frame after frame, which a small fast object never does. Choosing the single best path through every candidate instead, then filling the gaps, recovers most of it — checked by eye against the actual ball.

Distance, speed and team shape Needs a fixed camera view to measure against the pitch In progress

These are the only stats that need the pitch itself. A box on screen has to become a position in metres, and that mapping is one fit to the painted lines — which holds exactly as long as the camera holds still. The maths is built and agrees between the Python pipeline and the browser. What is missing is an afternoon of footage shot the right way.

  • A camera on a tripod, not a phone in a hand
  • One clean frame with the pitch markings visible
  • The pitch measured with a tape, not assumed

The last one matters more than it looks: a school pitch is rarely painted to the Laws, and eight perfect clicks read against the wrong dimensions still came out 1.9 m adrift at the worst corner.
